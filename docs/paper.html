06 · Paper

Design as the objective

Why an LLM must be given the structure it should produce — and a blind, adversarial evaluation of a method that does, losses and null results included.

Eliezer Avihail · September 2026 · LaTeX source

Abstract

The argument in one paragraph

A language model optimizes the objective it is given, not the instructions it is handed. Coding agents are scored — in training, in benchmarks, and in the closing condition of every agent loop — on functional correctness. Architectural quality is therefore never an objective; it is at best a stylistic prior, and it loses to the immediate task. From the mechanics of autoregressive generation and preference optimization, three consequences follow: a quality goal must be stated as a checkable predicate, not an adjective; it must be checked at the moment a structural decision is made, because a model conditioned on an interface it has already written will rationalize rather than revise it; and the rationale must be externalized into durable artifacts, because the model is stateless across sessions.

What the evaluation found — stated up front, wins and non-wins together. Against a spec-driven baseline on three fresh products, aims wins first-round design quality on all three on one recurring axis (value objects, one-owner-per-rule); change absorption is tie / win / tie on the rubric-free count, and both arms pass every correctness trap. Across six adaptation experiments — one on a real open-source module — a no-method control is correct every time; the method's measurable edge is a design trajectory that improves across successive changes while the control flattens and densifies. Costs are 2.5–3× the baseline. Two results are reported as instrument failures rather than wins (a perfect score everywhere = a ceiling effect; the designs speak the rubric's own vocabulary), and a fourth product is one aims lost decisively — caught blind, in the home method.
§2 · Mechanism

Why a model produces the structure it produces

At inference there is no search over programs and no objective over the finished artifact — only the likelihood of the next token given everything already in context. Preference training rewards outputs that appear to satisfy the request. Under a loop whose terminal condition is it works, structure is never the thing being optimized. Three requirements follow.

1 · An adjective is not an objective

"Write clean code" adds tokens about good structure without adding a predicate the generation can fail. Structure becomes real only as an enumerable list of yes/no items applied to the output.

2 · Check at the decision

Once the model has emitted an interface, every later token is conditioned on it; asking "is this right?" afterwards invites a defence. The check belongs inside the design step, before the artifact conditions the rest of generation.

3 · Externalize the rationale

No memory between sessions; attention decays within one. The reasons for a boundary die with the context. File them beside the code — and, because a stale record is followed, flag it stale.

The default attractor is under-structuring: the training median is flat, working code, and under a correctness-terminated loop nothing pulls away from it. Over-engineering is a local cost; under-structuring spreads, because every later change routes around the missing seam.

§4 · The method

Roles, two documents, one instrument

A Guide sets one design objective per round and delegates to Workers; an opening round may fan out a panel of axis-focused Workers merged best-from-each, and every planning round ends with one mandatory review-and-revise cycle. Two principle documents define what "correct" means — Design principles (fifteen chapters, §0–§14) for building from nothing, and Add-feature principles (12 chapters) for changing existing code — and one measurement instrument reads them.

Severity mechanics — the tier of a chapter's worst failed item sets its ceiling and its weight.
Worst failed item in the chapterCeilingWeight
none10×1
cosmetic (S1)8×1
moderate (S2)7×2
structural (S3)5×4
precondition / correctness (S4)2×8
Grade = the weighted list; severity = a reported gate, not a cap. The grade is Σ(chapter × weight) / Σweight; beside it, any S4 ⇒ BLOCKED is reported, with the worst chapter and the (#S3,#S4) counts — never a bare number. Applicability is fixed at step 0 from the specification (rules R, change-axes X, cases C), so all arms of one product share the same applicable set and are comparable, while grades across different products are never read as one verdict. (An earlier form of the instrument also applied a global graded cap by min; it was removed because it double-counted one defect three times, replaced by the gate above, and applied retroactively to every prior experiment with both readings kept.)

The gap is the same gap three times: value objects and one-owner-per-rule, exactly the axis the principle document declares dominant. The one trap that discriminated (booking) was a concept-cram the baseline absorbed by giving one rule a second owner; aims kept it to one owner after its concept-fit pass — Consequence 2 firing on a real case, in the design, where it costs a paragraph.

Two readings that are not wins

Both arms passed every correctness trap on all three — the traps discriminated on structure, not raw correctness. And aims scored a perfect first-round grade on all three, with all three judges noting its designs speak the rubric's own vocabulary — a ceiling effect and a capture risk, reported in Threats.

Cost

aims averaged ~140k tokens, 30–40 tool calls, 12 min per product; the baseline ~55k, 4 calls, 3 min — 2.5–3×. Final design length was not proportional to quality; the extra spend buys structure, not volume.

A product the method lost. On a fourth product outside this set — a feature-based identifier carried from plants to minerals across two staged reveals, judged blind by the same protocol — the aims arm lost decisively. Its type model (Term | Quantity, a single point) could not represent an observed range the stage-2 card named ("6.5–7"): a precondition (S4) failure on exactly the full-input-space trap the method champions. The blind instrument scored the home arm below the baseline and marked it BLOCKED. It is the cleanest evidence the instrument is not a home referee — and the defect is a design fault caught at review rather than prevented at design, the asymmetry Consequence 2 names. A wording change to the subtractive tie-break, prompted by the loss, was withheld and A/B-tested first on a further unseen product; it did not reproduce the over-cut and was not adopted — a loss-prompted change must prove itself on a product it has not seen.
§5 · Results — Study 2

Changing code that already exists

Six experiments against a no-method control, using the brownfield principle set and its before-and-after measure — one on a real open-source module. "Parity" means both arms produced a correct, behaviour-preserving change.

Correctness parity, everywhere — on a change a strong model can hold in one session, the method is not needed for correctness, stated plainly because it bounds the claim. The edge is a trajectory, and only a trajectory shows it (experiment 4): aims improves at each step while the control densifies — rot accumulating while every step works, which is why a single-change benchmark cannot see it. Consistency over dogma held: on a plain-style base aims did not impose value objects. Continuity is a partial null: on code small enough to re-derive, the record adds legibility, not a different outcome.

§6 · Measurement

Why there is no deterministic benchmark

The only pair of runnable arms differing in one design decision — a named return type vs. a bare positional tuple, which a blind judge penalized as a data clump — put every deterministic metric against that judgement.

Halstead (vol / effort / diff)36 / 48 / 1.3336 / 48 / 1.33

Halstead and cyclomatic complexity are identical; Maintainability Index and SLOC favour the design the judge graded lower, because a named type costs lines and adds no branches. The metrics are not wrong — they measure surface volume, a different construct from concept fit, and here the two are anti-correlated. Nearly every property that makes a design absorb the next requirement registers in a volume metric as cost. Architectural evaluation is therefore an instrument of procedure, not of measurement.

§7 · Threats & honest bottom line

What the evaluation does not support

The judge shares the method's rubric

The primary threat. Mitigated but not removed: applicability and evidence are pinned to the spec, a reproduction is required per claim (which surfaced defects against the method, including the lost product above), and the rubric-free survival count is reported separately — where the reading is materially weaker (tie / win / tie).

Vocabulary capture & ceiling

All three judges noted the designs recite the rubric's vocabulary. And a perfect score on every product is an instrument that has lost the resolution to register the next regression — read as a rubric needing a harder product, not as a result.

No correctness advantage on one-session changes

Six experiments say the opposite, plainly. The method's value is structural discipline and durable records that compound across a sequence — not a one-shot correctness edge.

The record layer, in outcomes

Supported only in process and legibility so far, because every codebase tested was small enough to re-derive — exactly the regime where the record is predicted not to matter. The open frontier is scale, and it is a measurement problem before it is a method problem.

The finding held most firmly is the least flattering: there is no deterministic benchmark for what this work tries to improve, the classical metrics that resemble one are anti-correlated with it on the only data where a comparison was possible, and the judged instruments that replace them are only as sound as the adversarial procedure around them. The protocol, principle documents, sealed mappings and run logs are published — and a well-run refutation would be a good outcome.
