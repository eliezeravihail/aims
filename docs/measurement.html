03 · Measurement

Scoring design quality

Each of 18 principles scored 0–10 from binary structural sub-checks: score = 10 × passed / applicable. Severity of the worst failed check sets the weight and a ceiling. Every score below 10 carries a cited finding.

The scale

Severity → score ceiling → weight

Worst failed checkScore ceilingWeightExample
none — holds by construction10×1—
S1 cosmetic8×1a naming nit
S2 moderate7×2primitive obsession, a data clump, a dead abstraction
S3 high5×4mis-owned invariant, a leaked type, a shotgun seam
S4 severe / correctness2×8wrong result, an unenforceable rule, a security hole

Aggregate = a profile, not a number: severity-weighted average · worst principle · (#S3, #S4). Graded caps: any S2 ⇒ ≤ 8.5 · any S3 ⇒ ≤ 7.5 · any S4 ⇒ ≤ 5.0. Step 0: the rules (R) / change-axes (X) / acceptance cases (C) are pinned from the spec, never a design — identical for every arm.

Two projections · one honest limit

What the form is

One form, two views

Building: the fix-list — sub-10 rows, most-severe-first, cited, no aggregate shown, so the Worker fixes content not a number. Comparing designs: the full scored form + profile. Same measurement, different view — not two instruments.

Reliable, not a benchmark

Sub-checks buy reproducibility (a misjudged check moves a fraction, not a band). They do not buy external validity — no ground-truth benchmark for design quality exists, and deterministic metrics (below) measure a different construct.
