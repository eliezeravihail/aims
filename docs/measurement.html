03 · Measurement

Scoring design quality

Each chapter of the design principles scored 0–10 from its items, each a binary cited sub-check: score = 10 × passed / applicable. Severity of the worst failed item sets the chapter's weight and ceiling. Every score below 10 carries a cited finding.

The scale

Severity → score ceiling → weight

Worst failed checkScore ceilingWeightExample
none — holds by construction10×1—
S1 cosmetic8×1a naming nit
S2 moderate7×2primitive obsession, a data clump, a dead abstraction
S3 high5×4mis-owned invariant, a leaked type, a shotgun seam
S4 severe / correctness2×8wrong result, an unenforceable rule, a security hole

Reported as a profile, never a bare number: the grade (the severity-weighted average over chapters) · the worst chapter · (#S3, #S4) · and a gate beside them — any S4 ⇒ BLOCKED, meaning not shippable until that item is fixed. The gate is a fact about shippability, not a verdict on the design; there is no global cap folded into the grade, because a cap counted one defect three times (decisions/0018). Step 0: the rules (R) / change-axes (X) / acceptance cases (C) are pinned from the spec, never a design — identical for every arm.

The instrument

The rows

One row per chapter of design-principles.md; a chapter's items are its sub-checks. Most items are N/A for a given artifact and leave the denominator, so a typical review scores 15–25 applicable items across the chapters. The chapters and their items are deliberately not copied here — a copy of the list drifts the moment the list changes, which is how this page once described an instrument two generations out of date. The rules of filling the form are in measurement.md.

An item's severity follows its correctness class: a precondition failing — functional correctness, one owner per rule, a leaked seam, concurrency safety, security at a boundary — is S4, because the code is wrong rather than untidy. Everything else is S1–S3 by how far it spreads.

Two projections · one honest limit

What the form is

One form, two views

Building: the fix-list — sub-10 rows, most-severe-first, cited, no aggregate shown, so the Worker fixes content not a number. Comparing designs: the full scored form + profile, scored from the code, never from what a design says about itself. Passing the tests is a floor that earns nothing — two designs passing an identical suite scored 43 vs 16 (decisions/0021). Same measurement, different view — not two instruments.

Reliable, not a benchmark

Sub-checks buy reproducibility (a misjudged check moves a fraction, not a band). They do not buy external validity — no ground-truth benchmark for design quality exists, and deterministic metrics (below) measure a different construct.
