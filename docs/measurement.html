03 · Measurement

Scoring design quality, reproducibly

The same principles used to build are used to grade — only the how differs. Grading is a standardized assessment form: each principle scored 0–10, and the score derived from a handful of binary structural sub-checks rather than eyeballed.

The assessment form

From sub-checks to a score

STEP 0

Fixed inventory

Pin the rules (R), change-axes (X), and acceptance cases (C) from the spec — never from a design. Identical for every judge and every arm, so a re-run is comparable.

PER PRINCIPLE

Sub-checks → score

Decompose the principle into binary structural checks, each cited. score = 10 × passed / applicable. Any score below 10 requires a cited finding; a 10 cites where the principle holds by construction.

SEVERITY

Weight & cap

The worst failed check sets the weight (×1 / ×2 / ×4 / ×8) and a ceiling. A §13 correctness or §9 ownership fault can reach S4 and caps the whole design, however clean the rest.

The aggregate is a profile, not a bare number: a severity-weighted average, the worst principle, and the count of S3/S4 findings, with graded caps (an S2 caps ≤ 8.5, an S3 ≤ 7.5, an S4 ≤ 5.0). One structural fault cannot read as "sound."

One instrument, two projections

Judging vs the operational review

Judging / measurement

The full scored form plus the aggregate profile — the layer that legitimately produces a number, for comparing arms and benchmarking. This is what the experiments report.

Operational review (in the loop)

The same form's score-less projection: only the sub-10 rows, sorted most-severe-first, each with its principle and citation, and no aggregate grade — so the Guide's review stays a direction-feeding findings list, faithful to "never a score."

"Never a score" refined, not reversed. No composite verdict decides direction in the loop; the per-principle severity is only the key that orders the findings so the most material violation leads. The scored form lives in the measurement layer, never in the operational loop.
Why sub-checks

What actually absorbs judge noise

The instrument's precision is limited by judge calibration noise — the same design has scored 0.5 apart from two judges with no qualitative disagreement. The fix is not a bigger number.

Scale width does not help

A judge cannot reliably place a design at 73 vs 76, so widening a holistic band from 0–4 to 0–100 just turns a ±0.5 wobble into ±12 — the same ~10%. The maximum is a red herring.

Decomposition does

A principle's score is the mean of several independent binary checks, so a single misjudged check moves it by a fraction, not a whole band. Across §1–§18 that is dozens of near-deterministic 0/1 measurements whose errors cancel — and each sharp check ("does rule R have one owner — cite it") is one two judges agree on far more often than a holistic score.

The honest limit

A reliable instrument, not a benchmark

The form buys reliability — the same design yields the same profile across judges and runs. It does not buy external validity, because there is no external ground-truth benchmark for design quality to anchor against.

No external benchmark exists

Correctness benchmarks (does it pass the tests) are ground-truthable and abundant; design quality is contextual, multi-dimensional, and needs expert judgment that disagrees — so the field has only deterministic proxies or subjective judgment, neither a benchmark. The form's validity rests on the principles being sound.

Deterministic metrics measure something else

Run on the two ledger arms, coupling/complexity/Halstead were blind to the difference the rubric penalized (a named return type vs a bare tuple) — Halstead identical, and volume metrics even favored the weaker arm. They measure surface complexity, a different construct — which is why aims triangulates the form with change-survival and static metrics as separate signals that need not agree.
