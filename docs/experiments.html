Every result here is exploratory. The latest comparison showed that the design grades depend on the judge as much as on the design, so no grade on this page is a verdict on its own.

Latest

aims vs the real OpenSpec tool: the measurement did not hold

Three products, design only, compared twice on the same OpenSpec designs (OpenSpec 1.13.2). In round 1 the aims designs came from an earlier run and one judge scored each product. In round 2 aims was re-run at its current version in stricter isolation, and two judges of opposite leanings scored each product.

  • rounds The two rounds disagree. By the form, OpenSpec did better on the changed design in round 1 (3 of 3) and aims in round 2 (5 of 6).
  • judges Same designs, different grades. The unchanged OpenSpec designs were graded from 5.7 to 9.8 depending on the judge, and wrong-output failures found in round 2 had been passed in round 1.
  • blind Not blind in practice. All nine judges named the method behind each design correctly, each at 97% confidence.
  • measures The principle-free measure disagrees with the form. In round 2 the count of components reopened or discarded favoured OpenSpec in 5 of 6 readings.

Round 1: results-openspec-real.md · round 2: results-aims-real.md · the paper's reading: Paper.

Experiments as series

Each sequence, and what it changed

The experiments run in four series. Each is a chain: a run produces a reading, the reading forces a specific change to the method, and the next run tests under the change. The record below reads the chain forward — experiment → what changed — with every change linked to the decision that carries it.

Series 1 · The greenfield design contest — aims vs OpenSpec / plain

ExperimentReading
aims-vs-openspecaims lost — third on design quality, reopened the most on survival. No design advantage found.
aims-upgraded-rerunDecisive survival win; quality still third, but now the two judges agree why (not a taste split).
single-pass-assessment-rerunUnder neutral measurement, aims ties checkout and leads marketplace — no longer last.
principles-polish-vs-openspec (3 products)First run: aims won the form on all three, but the OpenSpec arm only imitated the tool. Re-run with the real tool, twice: the rounds disagree, and the same designs were graded 5.7–9.8 by judge.
marketplace-change-absorptionSelf-score tie; the blind judge tips a slight edge to OpenSpec (one reopen too generous).
→ What changed: the first pilot's no-advantage result was recorded honestly (0007, trail through 0010) and established the two-arm protocol with anonymized designs and separate judges. Judges splitting the verdict = a taste artifact → a single assessment form became the one measurement instrument (0012, 0014). §2 was rebalanced — the "describable second implementation" prediction-gate dropped, under-structuring ranked the primary failure (0013) — and operator prompt-anchoring removed. Under those, aims stopped placing last.

Series 4 · After a loss — review discipline

ExperimentReading
plant-mineral-idaims lost decisively — 6.63 (BLOCKED) vs 9.54 (CLEAR); an S4 §4/§1 design defect (a type model that can't represent an observed "6.5–7"), caught blind in the home method.
grade-rule-regradeThe new grade rule applied retroactively to every prior experiment: aims' plant→mineral arm is the only BLOCKED arm in the set.
s7-yagni-stated-capabilityNull — a candidate §7 wording, A/B'd on an unseen product, did not change the outcome; both arms kept the capability.
→ What changed: the loss exposed that the old global cap double-counted one defect three times → the grade is now the weighted list; severity is a reported gate (any S4 ⇒ BLOCKED) beside it, not a cap on it (0018). To keep the instrument credible after a loss it produced, it was adopted only with two guardrails: applied retroactively to the whole set (grade-rule-regrade) and both readings kept — so the rule sharpens the loss, not softens it. And the wording change the loss suggested was not trusted on its own round: A/B'd on an unseen product, it came back null, so it was not folded in. A loss-prompted fix that doesn't prove itself stays out.

Reading the numbers

Protocol & confounds

What makes a run count

Two arms (aims vs a control) build the same product across staged reveals with a passive oracle; separate judges grade anonymized designs against the same inventory; every claim carries a reproduction or file:line. Anonymized is not blind: in the latest comparison every judge recognized the methods.

Why no single grade is a verdict

The judge spread is up to 4 points on the same design (5.7–9.8), not the ±0.5 once assumed · the form shares its source with the method · maturity asymmetry inflates the marketplace margin · n = 1 per product, product-dependent.

On the record: an earlier run showed aims last on checkout. With operator prompt-anchoring removed and §2 rebalanced, it did not reproduce — aims tied checkout and led marketplace. Both corrections contributed; at n = 1 they don't separate.
